## 0. The Core Positioning Decision

Every scheduler on the market — Buffer, Later, Hootsuite, even upstream Postiz — was designed for **one brand posting to its own channels**, then retrofitted for people who manage *other people's* brands: per-channel fees bolted on, "workspaces" bolted on, approvals never built at all. That retrofit is the source of every documented pain in our research: the per-channel tax, the WhatsApp-screenshot approvals, the renewal shock, the missing client boundaries.

So we do not position as a better scheduler. We position around the one structural thing we can own:

> **The client is the atomic unit of the product — not the post, not the channel.**

That single idea generates everything below: workspaces per client, approvals per client, reports per client, pricing that doesn't grow per channel, and messaging that speaks to *managers of other people's social media* rather than "everyone with an Instagram."

**What we deliberately do NOT position on:**
- **"AI-powered"** — table stakes in 2026; the research shows AI writing is a checkbox, and Ocoya proves "AI + scheduling" alone is a $726K lifestyle business. AI appears inside feature copy, never as identity.
- **"All-in-one marketing workspace / marketing OS"** — unearned at our stage (Business Understanding Report §3) and it dilutes the wedge.
- **"Cheapest"** — Metricool owns cheap. We are *fairly priced*, which is different: we win the pricing conversation on structure (flat vs punitive), not on the lowest number.
- **Open-source as identity** — that's upstream's turf. Open core appears once, as a trust proof ("your data is never hostage"), never as a headline.

## 1. Unique Value Proposition

**Primary UVP (one line):**

> **[Product] is the social media platform built for people who run social media for other people.**

**Expanded (the three-legged stool — every piece of copy stands on at least one leg):**

1. **Client-first structure** — every client gets a workspace, an approval link, and a report. No more screenshots in WhatsApp, no more clients seeing each other's content, no more reporting day.
2. **Pricing that doesn't punish growth** — flat monthly price, generous channel limits. Landing your next client makes you money; it doesn't raise your software bill.
3. **Reliability you can see, support that's human** — a public publishing success rate, alerts within a minute of any failure, and a founder answering support in under two hours. Monthly billing, cancel anytime — we re-earn you every month.

> For **boutique social media agencies and freelance social media managers** who run social for multiple client brands, **[Product]** is the **client-first social media management platform** that gives every client its own workspace, approval flow, and flat price — unlike **Buffer, Later, and Hootsuite**, which were built for single brands and charge you more for every channel and every client you win.

Category claim: we say **"client-first social media management"** — close enough to the known category that buyers instantly get it, distinct enough to make comparisons run on our axis (client workflow) instead of theirs (feature count).

### Voice and tone
Calm, precise, a little dry. Short declarative sentences. Numbers over adjectives. We sound like Stripe's docs, not like a marketing tool's homepage.

**Words we use:** clients, workspaces, approvals, flat, publish, reliable, yours.
**Words we ban:** revolutionize, supercharge, 10x, all-in-one, game-changing, "AI-powered" as a headline, unleash, effortless, seamless (show it instead), "the only tool you'll ever need."

**The one-line brand test:** if a sentence could appear on Buffer's homepage, cut it.

## 7. Emotional Messaging vs. Functional Messaging

The buyer is a founder-operator whose *name* is on every client outcome. The functional sale is money and time; the emotional sale is **professional dignity and control**.

**Emotional register (use in hero lines, community posts, founder content):**
- *Relief:* "The 3pm post went out. You didn't have to check."
- *Professional pride:* "Send clients an approval link, not a screenshot. Feel like the agency you are."
- *Control/safety:* "You'll know before the client knows."
- *Being on their side:* "Your tool shouldn't take a cut of your growth."
- *Anti-betrayal (the Later/Hootsuite trauma):* "Monthly billing. Cancel anytime. Renewal price = the price on the website. That shouldn't be a differentiator, but here we are."

**Functional register (use in comparison pages, pricing page, cold email, demos):**
- "$240/mo → $[X]/mo at 40 channels. Same channels."
- "Approval turnaround measured in hours, not screenshot-chase days."
- "99.X% publish success, alert within 60 seconds of any failure."
- "Migration done for you in an afternoon; parallel-run for two weeks before you cancel anything."

**Mixing rule:** emotional headline, functional proof directly beneath. Never two emotional lines in a row (reads like marketing); never functional-only pages (reads like a spec sheet — that's how a scheduler sounds, and we're not allowed to sound like a scheduler).

## 8. Pricing Positioning

*(Structure and narrative only — exact numbers follow the deferred pricing research, per PRICING_PHILOSOPHY.)*

1. **The frame: "the anti–per-channel tool."** We never present price without presenting the competitor math next to it. Our pricing page is also our best comparison page — the calculator lives there.
2. **Two tiers mirror the two ICPs.** Freelancer tier (~$19–29 zone): framed **per client** — "about $7 per client per month" beats "$29/mo" for Dana. Agency tier (~$79–129 zone): framed **as margin** — "one flat line item that stays flat; your next client is pure margin."
3. **Billing kindness as a stated feature, not fine print.** Monthly by default, cancel-anytime in the nav-visible copy, "renewal price = sticker price, always" as an explicit promise. In a market where Later holds a 1.3/5 Trustpilot on billing and Hootsuite renewals jump 40–65%, *the billing policy is a product feature* — position it like one.
4. **Premium-fair, not cheap.** Against Metricool ($36–53) we don't compete on the number; the copy is honest: "If scheduling is all you need, cheaper tools exist. If clients approve your work and your name is on the result, this is what those hours are worth."
5. **What we never do:** per-channel fees, per-seat fees for the agency tier (unlimited team members is a weapon — Sendible/Sprout charge per user), paid add-ons for core platforms (Vista Social's +$29 for X is their most-hated decision — we name that pattern and reject it publicly).

## 9. Competitive Differentiation ("why us" — by competitor class)

**Vs. Buffer / Later / Publer (per-channel pricers, single-brand DNA):**
> They price like your growth is their revenue. Every client you win raises your bill; approvals and client boundaries were never built. [Product] is structured around clients — and flat.

**Vs. Hootsuite / Sprout / Agorapulse (per-seat enterprise):**
> Built for one brand's marketing department: seats, contracts, renewals that arrive 40–65% higher. You're not a department. Flat price, unlimited team, monthly billing, no procurement.

**Vs. Metricool / SocialBee / Sendible (mid-market flat-tier):**
> The closest fight, so we out-position on the two things they lack: a real approval workflow (their client features are tacked on) and visible reliability + human support (Metricool's 1.8/5 Trustpilot is a billing-and-support story). We are "the one your clients touch."

**Vs. Planable (approval specialist):**
> They proved approvals are worth paying for — then charge $33 *per client workspace* and sell publishing/analytics as add-ons. Our approval flow is included, and the price doesn't multiply by client count. (Watch them closely; they're the competitor whose existence most validates our wedge.)

**Vs. AI-first tools (Ocoya, Predis, FeedHive):**
> They sell content generation with scheduling attached — credit meters, single-brand DNA. AI drafting is inside [Product] too; it's just not the point. Nobody's client relationship was ever saved by a caption generator.

**Vs. upstream Postiz (the awkward one — never dodge):**
> Same open-source heart, different product and different promise: client workspaces and approvals it doesn't have, a public reliability record, support answered by humans in hours (not a Discord), and a roadmap accountable to paying agencies. Self-hosting the free core is genuinely great for developers — and we say so. Agencies don't want to run servers; they want the 3pm post to go out.

**The differentiation summary in one sentence (for every "why you?" moment):**
> *"Everyone else built a scheduler for brands and stretched it to fit agencies. We built the agency tool first."*

The last line of the page routes them there deliberately.

## 11. Consistency Rules (so the positioning survives contact with reality)

1. **Every headline names clients or client-work.** If a page's H1 could describe posting your own memes, rewrite it.
2. **Every price appears next to a competitor's math.** We never let our number float context-free.
3. **AI never leads.** It may appear from the third scroll down.
4. **Every trust claim is a link** — to the reliability dashboard, the changelog, the support SLA, the billing promise. Claims without links are what schedulers do.
5. **We name competitors politely and specifically.** Vague "other tools" copy reads as fear; precise, fair comparisons read as confidence — and this audience fact-checks.
6. **The upstream question gets one calm paragraph everywhere it arises** (docs, FAQ, Reddit) — the §9 wording, verbatim. Consistency is what makes it a non-story.
7. **Quarterly test:** re-read the homepage and ask, *"could Buffer ship this page?"* The day the answer is yes, the positioning has eroded — fix it that week.
